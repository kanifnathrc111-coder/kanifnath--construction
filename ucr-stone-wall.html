What is UCR wall construction?

UCR stands for uncoursed rubble masonry: irregular stones are fitted together and bedded in cement mortar without being laid in regular level courses. Large stones go on the face, smaller stones and chips fill the gaps, and through stones tie the two faces together. The same work is also called random rubble or rubble stone masonry. A UCR wall is used where stone is available locally and a robust, plain wall is needed.

UCR, coursed rubble and dry stone: the differences

TypeHow the stone is laidTypical use
UCR (uncoursed rubble)Irregular stones in mortar, no continuous level coursesFoundations, plinths, retaining and boundary walls
Coursed rubbleRoughly levelled at intervals, more regular appearanceVisible walls where a tidier face is wanted
Dry rubbleNo mortar; stability comes from stone interlockLimited uses; confirm with the engineer

Where UCR walls are used

  • Retaining walls beside plots, roads and slopes (with weep holes and back drainage as designed)
  • Foundations and plinth walls
  • Compound and boundary walls
  • Drainage structures, culvert sides and bank protection, where specified

How a UCR wall is built

  1. Set out and prepare the foundationMark the alignment and levels from the drawing; excavate to the specified depth and prepare the base.
  2. Sort the stoneSeparate face stones, filling stones and long bond (through) stones. Stone should be hard, clean and free of cracks.
  3. Bed the stone in mortarSet large stones first, fit each stone so it sits firmly, and avoid continuous vertical joints.
  4. Pack and bondFill every gap with mortar and stone chips, and place through stones at intervals as specified.
  5. Provide drainageFor retaining walls, form weep holes and back drainage exactly as shown in the drawing.
  6. Cure and finishKeep the masonry damp as specified, then complete any pointing or finishing.

Quality points that matter on site

  • Poor packing and hollow interiors are a main cause of weak rubble masonry.
  • Wide mortar joints, loose face stones and missing through stones weaken the wall.
  • Mortar mix, wall thickness and foundation depth come from your drawing or engineer, not from this website.
  • Retaining walls without working drainage can bulge or crack.

What affects the rate of UCR wall work

We do not publish a standard rate, because the same wall can cost very differently on different sites. The main factors are:

  • Volume of masonry and wall height
  • Foundation depth and excavation
  • Stone, cement and sand supply and haul distance
  • Access, scaffolding and working conditions
  • Finish: plain, pointed or faced

If you are choosing between wall types, compare with gabion retaining walls and stone pitching. The choice is a design decision for your engineer.

Frequently asked questions about UCR walls

What does UCR mean in construction?

UCR stands for uncoursed rubble masonry. Irregular stones are bedded in cement mortar without being laid in regular horizontal courses. It is also called random rubble masonry or rubble stone masonry, depending on the region and the specification.

Is UCR the same as random rubble masonry?

In everyday site use the terms overlap. UCR normally means the stones are not brought to level courses, while coursed rubble is levelled at intervals. Your drawing or BOQ should name the exact masonry type so the quotation matches it.

Can you give a UCR wall rate per cubic metre or square foot?

We do not publish a fixed rate. The rate depends on the volume of masonry, wall height and foundation depth, who supplies stone, cement and sand, access, and finish. Send the drawing or BOQ and the site location and we will quote for that work.

Can I supply the stone and materials myself?

Yes. State who provides stone, cement, sand, scaffolding and water, and the quotation will list the scope on that basis. Labour-only and supply-with-labour are both possible to enquire about.

Is design or drawing included?

No, unless agreed in writing. We work to your drawings and specification. For retaining walls, the structural design and drainage details should come from the project engineer.

Should I choose a UCR wall or a gabion wall?

It is a design decision that depends on height, ground conditions, drainage, stone availability, appearance and budget. Both are covered on this site: see gabion retaining walls. Ask your engineer, and share the drawing for either option when you enquire.
