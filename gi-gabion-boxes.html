What does GI mean for a gabion box?

GI stands for galvanised iron. In gabion work it refers to steel wire that carries a zinc coating to slow down corrosion. The zinc layer is the corrosion protection, so the coating requirement written into your specification matters as much as the wire diameter.

Where GI gabion boxes are commonly considered

  • Retaining walls and embankment support where site conditions are not chemically aggressive
  • Riverbank, stream and canal protection, subject to flow conditions and design
  • Erosion control on slopes and around drainage structures
  • Landscape and boundary walls

Service life depends on the coating, the water and soil chemistry, abrasion and exposure. We do not state a design life on this website; the designer or project authority should set the coating requirement for the actual site.

GI or PVC-coated: how to decide

Comparison of GI and PVC-coated gabion boxes
Consideration GI (zinc-coated) PVC-coated over zinc
Corrosion protection Zinc coating on the wire PVC layer over zinc-coated wire
Typical consideration General civil applications More demanding or aggressive environments
Cost Usually lower per unit Usually higher per unit
Decision basis Project specification and site conditions Project specification and site conditions

This is a general comparison, not a design recommendation. See PVC-coated gabion boxes for the coated option.
