What to include in a gabion labour enquiry

For a useful discussion, describe whether the job involves gabion box assembly, positioning, tying, stone filling, on-site labour or a broader installation-related scope. Final responsibilities need to be confirmed for each project.

01

Work scope

Explain the tasks expected from the labour team and what will be handled by the client or other contractors.

02

Quantity & dimensions

Share approximate box counts, wall length and height, mattress area or BOQ references where available.

03

Site conditions

Include location, access, unloading arrangements, terrain and any timing or safety constraints.

04

Duration & programme

Share the expected start window, work duration and coordination requirements.

Supply-only versus labour scope

Gabion product supply and on-site labour are different quotation components. State whether you already have the boxes, stones and drawings, or whether you are enquiring about additional supply. Material, tools, transport, supervision and site preparation responsibilities should be stated clearly in the written quotation.

How the enquiry process works

  1. Share the work scope, quantity and project location.
  2. Provide drawings, BOQ, site photos or technical requirements if available.
  3. Clarify material responsibility, labour responsibility, access and expected duration.
  4. Review a written quotation with inclusions, exclusions, price basis and payment terms.

Common questions

Can I request labour-only pricing?
Yes. Describe the tasks and confirm which materials, tools, transport and site facilities are being provided by each party. Pricing can be assessed once the scope is clear.
What details are needed for a gabion labour quote?
Include site location, work quantities, dimensions, drawings or BOQ if available, access and unloading details, expected duration and proposed start schedule.
Is labour availability guaranteed across India?
No automatic availability or nationwide deployment is promised. Site location, timing, scope and feasibility must be reviewed before confirming.
