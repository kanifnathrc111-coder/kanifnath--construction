What is a gabion mattress?

A gabion mattress is a low-profile, rectangular mesh unit - much shallower than a standard gabion box - divided into cells by internal diaphragms and filled with stone. Placed side by side it forms a flexible, permeable lining that protects the surface beneath it.

Typical applications

  • Channel beds and sides
  • Riverbank and stream-bank lining
  • Slope surface protection against surface erosion
  • Scour protection at the toe of a gabion wall or around structures

Selection and thickness depend on flow velocity, slope, soil and the project design. Share the design details rather than relying on general guidance.

Supply and installation

Mattress placing involves preparing the surface, laying the units, lacing adjacent units, filling with stone and closing the lids. If you need this as labour or as a construction scope, see gabion labour supply and slope protection.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a gabion box and a gabion mattress?

A mattress is shallow and wide and is used as a lining or apron; a box is deeper and is stacked to form a wall or block.

Do I need geotextile under the mattress?

Often the design calls for a filter layer. State whether it is required and who supplies it.
